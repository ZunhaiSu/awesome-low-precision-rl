# Current editorial assessment

The 22 October 2026 edition retains the original paper template and the 20 October literature snapshot. Its central contribution is a connected account of how low-precision execution changes learning, supported by component-level classification and configuration-specific evidence.

The framework now gives four primary interventions clear roles: computation alignment, numerical fidelity and update visibility, gradient estimation, and policy-update control. Scheduling remains a complementary topic. All 29 focal studies and their section mappings remain available.

The strongest improvement is the connection between mechanism and comparison. Calculated rounding and score-centering examples explain what TRACE and SC change. The TRIAGE discussion distinguishes eligibility, normalized influence and bounded repair. Execution and comparison tables expose learner arithmetic, W/A/KV scope, missing baselines and checkpoint rules. The companion comparison page preserves those details alongside primary-source links.

This remains a focused narrative survey. The corpus is curated rather than exhaustive, experimental evidence is heterogeneous, and the survey independently reproduces none of the reported methods. The selected comparison records do not constitute a unified benchmark. Broader training-seed extraction, controlled cross-method reproduction and validation of predictive diagnostics remain future work.
