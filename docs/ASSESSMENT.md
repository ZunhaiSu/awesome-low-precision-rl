# Current editorial assessment

The survey retains eight main sections, four primary intervention classes, the original template, and the 20 October 2026 literature snapshot. Its argument follows the GRPO learning loop through numerical disagreement, estimation and update mechanisms, intervention design, and evidence on learned quality and total cost.

Section 3.4 now distinguishes four levels of diagnosis: numerical or distributional agreement, estimator reliability, update distortion, and learning outcomes. It explains why an absolute log gap, a sampled KL estimate, a full-vocabulary conditional KL, and empirical ESS cannot be interpreted interchangeably. Appendix E maps selected measurements across all nineteen core studies. Versioned source records preserve the policy pair, measurement location, and interpretation boundary rather than substituting a shared formula for unreported details.

The framework draws selectively on distinctions in primary papers' related work: mismatch source, intervention target, and tested setting remain separate views. Within update control, boundary placement, batch reliability, geometry, directional influence, and candidate acceptance clarify the decisions being changed without creating additional primary classes. The manuscript also separates QAT forward simulation from native arithmetic and backward precision.

These additions make the comparison more precise, but do not turn it into a benchmark. The corpus remains a focused narrative collection of twenty-nine contributions. Evidence is source-reported and heterogeneous; no independent reproduction, universal ranking, or calibrated scalar measure of harmful mismatch is established. A future benchmark would need matched measurement specifications and separate evidence for each link from disagreement to learning.
